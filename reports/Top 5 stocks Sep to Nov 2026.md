# A provisional five-stock basket for Sep 28 to Nov 20, 2026

This report gives a **provisional five-stock basket**, drawn from a 21-name shortlist, for a paper test of whether estimate revisions and momentum can beat the S&P 500 from Mon Sep 28 to Fri Nov 20, 2026. It does not establish that these are the five best stocks. Scored with one rule for risk and one for catalysts across all 21 names (V3), the rule-based basket is **Travelers (TRV), JPMorgan Chase (JPM), Valero (VLO), Tenet Healthcare (THC) and Micron (MU)**, averaging 3.62 out of 5. No basket is privileged beyond that rule. Under random one-point changes to the scores, TRV and JPM are selected in 86% and 84% of runs and VLO in 72%. THC (52%) and MU (44%) are unstable, and the second AI slot rotates among MU, DELL, ANET, APH and NVDA. These are model-selection frequencies, not probabilities of beating the index. The earlier V2 basket, **NVDA, TRV, APH, JPM and THC**, relied on a risk score for Nvidia that V2 itself called hard to defend and on a judgment override of Arista. It is kept below as an alternative, together with the basket that follows if Nvidia confirms a Nov 17 report date. That matters because revisions and price momentum are the only signals with robust evidence at a two-month horizon. Post-earnings drift has largely disappeared in large caps. The edge is modest. We assume a single stock beats the index over about two months only **45–48% of the time** (an unvalidated estimate), and an equal-weight five-stock basket still carries roughly ±5–7% of one-standard-deviation relative noise. So this is a probability tilt, not a forecast. The backdrop is fragile. The Fed hiked on Sep 16, the 10-year Treasury is near 5.2%, oil is around $92 on the Hormuz standoff, market breadth is at record-weak levels, and "long semis" is the most crowded trade. The basket's weakest scenario is a further yield spike, which would hit JPM and TRV together. The runners-up are **Arista (ANET), Dell (DELL), Micron (MU), Valero (VLO) and Cenovus (CVE)**. This is research, not investment advice.

**Read this before using any number in this report.** The finance pages that hold quotes, estimates and calendars (Yahoo Finance, CNBC, stockanalysis, MarketBeat, Zacks, GuruFocus, FactSet, SEC, BLS and BEA) were blocked by the research environment's network proxy. **Nearly every price, estimate, date and valuation figure here comes from search-engine result snippets, not full-page reads.** Snippets can mix dates or paraphrase wrongly, and the notes rejected several stale ones. **Re-verify every price, earnings date and estimate with a live quote tool before acting on this report**, especially the Sep 25 closing prices, the unconfirmed earnings dates (NVDA, APH, THC) and the "what proves it wrong" price levels. ## A coin flip sets the bar; revisions and momentum tilt it

Start with the base rate, because it is less favorable than most stock-picking implies. On average, just under half of S&P 500 members beat the index in a calendar year. In narrow, megacap-led years the share falls sharply: **44% in 2025 and 28% in 2024** ([Apollo Academy](https://www.apolloacademy.com/percentage-of-sp-500-stocks-outperforming-the-index/)). Our notes infer a **45–48% base rate for a single stock beating the index over about two months**. Shorter horizons suffer less skew drag, but 2026's weak breadth lowers the odds for the average stock. That figure is our inference; no published two-month statistic was found. Any selection edge has to be measured against that roughly coin-flip bar.

The signals with the best evidence at this horizon are estimate revisions and intermediate-term price momentum. Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok found top-minus-bottom decile spreads over the following six months of about **8.8% for price momentum and 7.7% for analyst revisions**, and each signal carried independent information ([J. Finance 1996](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2329242); these figures come from the canonical paper and were not re-fetched this session). A practitioner update finds that revision breadth and magnitude still correlate with relative returns over one to six months ([Mill Street Research](https://www.millstreetresearch.com/do-analyst-estimate-revisions-still-help-forecast-relative-stock-returns/)). Six-month momentum historically earned about 1% a month ([Jegadeesh & Titman 1993](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x)). It is prone to crashes when losers rebound sharply ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)). Stocks also earn a premium in months when they are expected to report ([Frazzini & Lamont](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=986940)). That supports favoring names with an earnings date inside the window.

Post-earnings-announcement drift is the signal to discount. Martineau concludes that "for large stocks, PEAD has basically been gone since 2006" ([Critical Finance Review / SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3111607)). A big August or September beat by itself therefore earns little credit here. This is why the recent beat-and-raise names DELL, Everpure and Snowflake score lower on catalyst than on revisions.

The momentum signal carries a 2026-specific warning. In 2026, momentum has meant AI semis. The MTUM ETF was up about 27% YTD in early July, then fell nearly 7% in a week, and five stocks accounted for more than half of its gain ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/mtum-owns-winners-july-could-191043086.html)). In May, Goldman put momentum positioning at the **100th percentile of the past five years** ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/etfs/specialty-etfs/26/05/52414461/momentum-etfs-tumble-as-overcrowded-ai-trade-unwinds-but-goldman-says-history-favors-buying-the-dip)). This is why the portfolio caps AI hardware at two names.

Volatility decides how much a pick can drift from the index by chance. The table below converts annualized relative volatility to an eight-week one-standard-deviation band, using √(8/52) ≈ 0.39. The calculation is ours, not a sourced figure.

| Annualized relative volatility | Typical stocks | 8-week 1-s.d. relative move |
|---|---|---|
| ~20% | Megacap platforms, staples, banks | about ±8% |
| ~30% | Typical S&P 500 member | about ±12% |
| 45–60% | Semis, VRT, MU, SMCI | about ±18–24% |

This is why the scorecard weights downside risk at 15%, where a score of 5 means the lowest risk. A high-beta AI name needs a much larger edge to justify the same slot as a bank or insurer.

The scorecard is unchanged from the handoff. Each factor is scored 0–5 and weighted as follows: estimate revisions 25%, catalyst strength and timing in the window 20%, momentum and relative strength 20%, downside risk 15% (5 = lowest risk), valuation room 10%, and structural thesis 10%.

## How fragile the ranking is

The scorecard rests on judgment calls, and several inputs are stand-ins. Revisions scores use guidance raises, target changes and Zacks ranks because measured revision histories were unavailable. So we tested how much the selection moves when those inputs change. The test applies the report's selection rule mechanically: rank by weighted score, break ties in favor of an in-window report and then lower risk, and apply both caps. The script and its full output are in `analysis/sensitivity.py` and `analysis/sensitivity_output.txt`.

**The mechanical rule does not pick APH.** It selects NVDA, TRV, ANET, JPM and THC. APH is in the report's basket only through the judgment override described above.

**NVDA depends on two uncertain inputs:**

| Scenario | NVDA score | Mechanical basket |
|---|---|---|
| As scored | 3.75 | NVDA, TRV, ANET, JPM, THC |
| NVDA risk 4 → 3 | 3.60 | DELL, TRV, ANET, JPM, THC |
| NVDA risk 4 → 2 | 3.45 | DELL, TRV, ANET, JPM, THC |
| NVDA reports Nov 25, after the window (catalyst 3 → 2, the same treatment as DELL) | 3.55 | DELL, TRV, ANET, JPM, THC |
| Both: risk 2 and Nov 25 report | 3.25 | DELL, TRV, ANET, JPM, THC |

NVDA's selection therefore depends on the unresolved report date as well as the risk judgment. If NVDA confirms a date after Nov 20 before the window starts, DELL takes its slot under the report's own rules.

**Weight changes.** Moving any one factor weight by 5 percentage points, and rescaling the others, changed the basket in 7 of 12 cases. Every change was inside the AI slots: NVDA, ANET, APH and DELL rotate, while TRV, JPM and THC stayed in all 12.

**Single-point changes.** Across all 252 one-point moves (21 names × 6 factors × up or down), the basket changed in 24. Twenty-two of those rotated the AI slots. The other two were THC losing a point on revisions or catalyst.

**Random noise.** We moved every sub-score by −1, 0 or +1 (probabilities 25%, 50%, 25%) and re-ran the selection 20,000 times:

| Ticker | Selected in % of draws |
|---|---|
| TRV | 91% |
| JPM | 77% |
| THC | 64% |
| NVDA | 52% |
| ANET | 49% |
| APH | 43% |
| VLO | 30% |
| CVE | 29% |
| DELL | 28% |
| MU | 18% |

The exact report basket came up in only 6% of draws, and the most common basket (the mechanical one) in 7%. These are **model-selection frequencies**, not probabilities of beating the index. They say that, within this scoring framework, TRV is selected far more reliably than THC, which drops out of 36% of runs. The tests only perturb scores inside the framework. They do not cover systematically wrong source data, other definitions of risk or momentum, scoring errors that move together across the AI names, other portfolio constraints or a wider universe. The claim that these are *the* five best stocks is not supported. The data support a shortlist of hypotheses with low confidence in the exact order.

**What the scorecard does not answer.** Rising estimates are a documented predictive signal, but the scorecard never asks what the price already expects. TRV's quiet hurricane season is public. JPM's raised guidance is already in the higher consensus. NVDA's relative return depends on beating expectations embedded in a $5.4T valuation. The picks rest on the statistical tendency of revisions and momentum to persist, not on evidence that consensus is wrong.

The basket has no oil exposure, so the direct effect is neutral to mildly positive. Lower yields would help TRV's book value and JPM's multiple, and the index would lose its energy leadership.

The indirect risk is a momentum reversal. A bond rally that sparks a rush into laggards is the most plausible momentum-crash trigger in this window. THC, the basket's highest-momentum name at RSI 75, is the most exposed. "Short Treasuries" is the second most crowded trade in the Fund Manager Survey, which could amplify the reversal.

**A further yield spike** is the Fund Manager Survey's top tail risk and the basket's weakest scenario. JPM already fell 3.4% on the day the 10-year crossed 5%. TRV loses book value through bond marks. NVDA and APH carry long-duration multiples. Only THC, on a 12.6x multiple, is relatively insulated. Watch the Oct 14 CPI, the Oct 29 PCE and GDP, and the Nov 4 refunding.

**A crack in AI capex** would test the NVDA and APH slots, 40% of an equal-weight basket. Oracle's Sep 24 force-majeure notice was the first dated crack ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/oracle-sends-force-majeure-notice-on-its-new-mexico-stargate-data-center/)). The GLJ note on GEV showed the whole AI-power complex trading as one factor that day: ETN fell 7% and PWR 4% ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/14/ge-vernova-sinks-9-as-glj-research-starts-at-sell-with-470-target-eaton-drops-7-quanta-services-falls-4/)). The index itself is heavily AI-weighted, with NVDA alone about 8%. So an AI sell-off hits the benchmark too. Whether the basket's 60% non-AI weight helps relative returns in that scenario depends on the basket's total AI sensitivity against the benchmark's. That includes indirect exposure, such as JPM's and TRV's losses in an AI-led risk-off. We have not measured it, so the cap is a sensible precaution, not demonstrated protection.

**Crowding** is concentrated in semis: 53% of managers call them the most crowded trade, and hedge-fund net exposure was at the 98th percentile in July. It also shows in THC's overbought reading. For banks, the Fund Manager Survey's contrarian call is "short banks". No verified short-interest data exist for most picks.

**Event clustering** adds to the risk. Two picks report within four days of each other, and three more sit in the Oct 27–30 week. Earnings-gap risk is therefore concentrated rather than spread across the window.

## Grading the picks on Nov 20 and Nov 23

**Official basket.** The V3 rule-based basket (TRV, JPM, VLO, THC, MU) is the pre-registered official basket, because it follows the stated rules. The V2 basket and the NVDA-confirmed variant are graded as supplementary results.

**Fixed window.** The official result uses the **Mon Sep 28 open** to the **Fri Nov 20 close** for every stock and for the benchmark. Do not substitute Sep 25 closes. That would change the experiment. Use one entry convention for the whole basket: if the Sep 28 open is unavailable for any stock, switch every stock and the benchmark to the Sep 28 close. Report any mixed-convention result only as a separate note.

**Benchmark.** Measure the S&P 500 total return with SPY (or the S&P 500 Total Return index) at exactly the same timestamps.

**Dividends.** Count a dividend only if its **ex-dividend date falls between Sep 29 and Nov 20 inclusive**, which means a Sep 28 buyer is entitled to it. Payment inside the window is not enough. For example, a dividend paid in October to holders of record in mid-September belongs to the seller. Apply the same rule to SPY's distributions.

Grade on four levels:

1. **Per-pick excess return.** Each stock's total return minus the benchmark's.
2. **Hit rate, with the right chance model.** Using the assumed (unvalidated) 45–48% base rate and assuming independent picks, the chance of at least four of five beating the index is **13–16%**, and of all five **2–2.5%**. The picks share exposures (yields, AI capex), so the true chance of a lucky cluster is higher. **Four or five winners would be encouraging, not proof of skill.** One eight-week window cannot establish an edge either way.
3. **Basket excess return.** The equal-weight basket against the benchmark. The ±5–7% band is a rough one-standard-deviation estimate, not a measured covariance or a significance threshold. A result outside it is a large outcome, not evidence of skill.
4. **Process check.** For each pick, record whether its "what proves it wrong" signal fired and whether the stock then underperformed. A pick can beat the index for the wrong reason or lose despite a correct thesis.

**Supplementary checks, reported separately from the official result:**

- **The V2 basket** (NVDA, TRV, APH, JPM, THC), the **NVDA-confirmed variant** (NVDA in place of MU), and the next names in V3 order (DELL, CVE, ANET, APH), graded the same way. One window cannot show whether the caps or the override helped, because selection and sector exposure change together. Treat these as descriptive only.
- **NVDA's report date.** Record the actual NVDA and THC report dates. If NVDA reports after Nov 20, note that its in-window catalyst did not happen. An optional Nov 30 re-mark of NVDA is a sensitivity check and never replaces the fixed-window result.

Running the grading on Mon Nov 23 captures the Nov 20 closing data after options expiration. A grading check is scheduled for that date.
