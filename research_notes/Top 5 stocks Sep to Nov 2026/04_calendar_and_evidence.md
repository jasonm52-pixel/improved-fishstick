## Q5. Evidence: which signals predict relative returns over about 2 months?

### Takeaway
Over a 2-month horizon, the most robust large-cap evidence supports three signals:
1. **Analyst estimate-revision momentum.** It works at 1–6 month horizons and is the least decayed of the signals.
2. **Intermediate (12-2 / 6-month) price momentum, skipping the most recent month**, because 1-month returns tend to reverse.
3. **An earnings-announcement premium** for stocks that report inside the window.

Classic PEAD is **largely gone for large caps since about 2006** (Martineau 2022), so "buy after a beat" should be weighted lightly. Momentum carries crash risk at sharp market reversals and has been very crowded in AI names in 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD)**
- **[training knowledge]** Bernard & Thomas (1989, *J. Accounting Research*): the extreme-SUE decile long/short earns about **4%+ abnormal return over the 60 trading days** after the announcement (about 18% annualized). The drift is concentrated in small firms and clusters around the next announcements — [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2491062)
- Martineau (2022, *Critical Finance Review*), "Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift": in modern markets prices fully reflect earnings surprises on announcement day. "**For large stocks, PEAD has basically been gone since 2006**," and it has recently disappeared for microcaps too — [SSRN 3111607](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3111607), [CFR](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0122)
- Counterpoint: recent work argues the drift survives when surprises are measured with text or richer signals (for example, combining press-release language with numbers) — [arXiv 2606.29734, "Fast Numbers, Slow Language"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29734), [arXiv 2509.24254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24254). These are working papers.

**Analyst estimate revisions**
- **[training knowledge]** Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok (1996, *J. Finance*, "Momentum Strategies"): over the following 6 months, the top-minus-bottom decile spreads were about **8.8% for past price momentum, about 7.7% for analyst revisions (REV6) and about 6.8% for SUE**. Revisions and price momentum each carry independent information, and the drift is concentrated around subsequent earnings announcements — [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2329242) / [DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05222.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05222.x)
- Practitioner update (Mill Street Research, "Do analyst estimate revisions (still) help forecast relative stock returns?"): revisions **breadth and magnitude each correlate with future relative returns over 1–6 month horizons** and reinforce each other — [Mill Street Research](https://www.millstreetresearch.com/do-analyst-estimate-revisions-still-help-forecast-relative-stock-returns/)
- Guerard (FactSet symposium chapter): earnings forecasts and revisions, price momentum and fundamentals as a combined stock-selection model — [FactSet PDF](https://go.factset.com/hubfs/Symposium%20Images/Guerard_EARNINGS%20FORECASTS%20AND%20REVISIONS,%20PRICE%20MOMENTUM,%20AND%20FUNDAMENTAL%20DATA.pdf?hsLang=en)
- Index level: revisions to near-term S&P 500 bottom-up forecasts explain **more than 10% of the variation in 3- and 6-month index returns** (1994–2023) — [Federal Reserve FEDS 2024-049](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2024049pap.pdf)
- Evidence that investors still underreact to forecast revisions (2025) — [Accounting & Business Research, 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00014788.2025.2545847)

**Price momentum, crashes and short-term reversal**
- **[training knowledge]** Jegadeesh & Titman (1993, *J. Finance*): 6-month formation and 6-month holding winners-minus-losers earns about **1% per month (about 12% per year)**. The strategy **loses about 7% on average in January** — [DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x)
- Daniel & Moskowitz (2016, *J. Financial Economics*, "Momentum Crashes"): momentum has negative skew and crashes in "panic states," meaning after market declines and with high volatility, when the market rebounds sharply. Losers (high beta) then rally. Example per AQR's summary: momentum fell about **73% over March–August 2009**. Momentum payoffs are forecastable, and volatility-scaling roughly doubles the Sharpe ratio — [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf), [AQR summary](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Momentum-Crashes)
- **[training knowledge]** Short-term reversal: stocks' prior-month returns tend to reverse the following month (Jegadeesh 1990, *J. Finance*: roughly **2% per month** extreme-decile spread; Lehmann 1990, *QJE*). This is why standard momentum uses 12-2 or 12-1 and skips the latest month. The effect is weaker in large caps and is mostly a liquidity-provision effect — [Jegadeesh 1990 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x)

**Seasonality and the earnings-announcement premium**
- Frazzini & Lamont (2007, NBER w13090 / AQR): stocks earn a **premium in months when they are expected to announce**. Monthly announcement strategies earn **about 7–18% per year** excess return, with Sharpe ratios above other anomalies. The premium is larger for stocks with high past announcement volume, consistent with attention-driven retail buying. Daily detail: about **+0.25% abnormal in the 9 days before** the announcement and **+0.30% in the 10 days after** — [SSRN 986940](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=986940), [NBER w13090](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13090/w13090.pdf), [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/library/working-papers/the-earnings-announcement-premium-and-trading-volume)
- The premium is also found globally — [Barber, De George, Lehavy & Trueman (JFE 2013), "The earnings announcement premium around the globe"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12002188). Savor & Wilson link it to systematic risk — [Savor & Wilson draft](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Draft20111215p_edited.pdf)
- Momentum winners have positive announcement returns tied to limited attention — [Review of Accounting Studies](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-009-9104-9)
- **[training knowledge]** Q4 seasonality: momentum's weakness is concentrated in **January**, when prior losers rebound as tax-loss selling reverses (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993, above). Momentum tends to be relatively strong in **November and December**, as tax-loss selling pressures losers (Grinblatt & Moskowitz 2004, *JFE*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(03)00175-2)). The Sep 28–Nov 20 window does **not** include January, so the known seasonal penalty to momentum does not apply.

### Inferences
- **Ranking of signals for this window (my synthesis):**
  1. **Positive, broad upward estimate revisions over the last 1–3 months.** This is the most relevant signal. Q3 2026 aggregate revisions were unusually positive (+1.2% vs a typical −2%, per 00_market_regime), so the relative signal should be measured vs sector peers.
  2. **Price momentum** (12-2 or 6-1), avoiding stocks with an extreme last-1-month spike.
  3. **The stock reports inside the window**, which captures the announcement premium. That is true for nearly the whole list.
  4. **PEAD from a big Q2 beat.** This is weak for large caps.
- **Crash-state check:** momentum crashes need a market decline followed by a sharp rebound led by losers. A Hormuz deal that lowers oil and yields would set off a rally in rate-sensitive laggards (small caps, equal-weight). That is the most plausible momentum-reversal trigger in this window, especially given crowding (see Q7).

### Gaps
- Precise post-2010 large-cap-only effect sizes for revisions and momentum were not retrieved (for example, S&P/MSCI factor index returns for the Oct–Nov seasonal).

## Q6. 8-week return dispersion and the base rate of beating the S&P 500

### Takeaway
The evidence that a single stock beats the index is **slightly below a coin flip**. On average just under 50% of S&P 500 members outperform in a year. In narrow, megacap-led years the share is much lower (28% in 2024, 44% in 2025), because of positive skew and cap-weight concentration. A ±12% one-standard-deviation 8-week **relative** return is reasonable for an average-volatility large cap (about 30% annualized active volatility). It is too small for high-beta AI hardware, where ±16–25% is more realistic, and too large for megacap defensives.

### Cited Findings
- On average just under 50% of S&P 500 stocks beat the index each calendar year. **44% did in 2025 and 28% in 2024.** Most constituents underperformed in 13 of the past 24 years — [Apollo Academy](https://www.apolloacademy.com/percentage-of-sp-500-stocks-outperforming-the-index/), [TKer](https://www.tker.co/p/most-sp500-constituents-underperform-positive-skew), [A Wealth of Common Sense, Oct 2025](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2025/10/how-many-stocks-outperform-the-stock-market/)
- Bessembinder: 4% of stocks account for all net US wealth creation since 1926. The median stock return is far below the mean because of positive skew — [Pareto Investor summary](https://paretoinvestor.substack.com/p/96-4-rule-the-extreme-reality-of-stock-market-returns), [Why Indexing Works (arXiv 1510.03550)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03550)

### Inferences (calculation, not sourced)
- An 8-week horizon is √(8/52) ≈ 0.39 of annual volatility. Applied to annualized relative (active) volatility:

| Annualized relative vol | Typical stocks | 8-week 1-s.d. relative move |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | megacap platforms, staples, banks | ≈ ±8% |
| 30% | typical S&P 500 member | ≈ ±12% |
| 45–60% | semis, SMCI, VRT, MU | ≈ ±18–24% |

- The ±12% claim is right for the median stock but understates risk for AI hardware. A single earnings event typically accounts for a large share (often a third or more) of 8-week idiosyncratic variance for these names.
- **Base rate for a single S&P 500 stock to beat the index over about 2 months: roughly 45–48%.** This is an inference: the annual rate is just under 50%, shorter horizons have less skew drag, and the current narrow, megacap-led breadth lowers the odds for the average stock. With 5 picks at average correlation, an equal-weight basket's chance of beating the index depends mostly on the edge of the signals above, not on diversification. Diversification narrows dispersion, roughly ÷√5 for idiosyncratic risk, to about ±5–7% for the basket.

### Gaps
- No direct published statistic on the 2-month (rolling 40-trading-day) share of S&P 500 members that beat the index was found.
- No 2026 measure of realized cross-sectional dispersion was found (for example, the S&P 500 Dispersion Index / DSPX level).

Momentum has been strong but crowded and prone to sharp drops in 2026. MTUM was up about 12% YTD in late April (about 8pp ahead of the S&P 500). A crowded-AI unwind hit momentum ETFs in May 2026. ### Inferences
- In 2026 momentum equals AI/semis concentration. A momentum tilt in Oct–Nov is effectively an AI-capex bet, carrying the same crowding and crash risk seen in May and July. For a *risk-aware* 5-stock pick, a practical approach is:
  - Combine momentum with **revisions**, which are fundamentally anchored.
  - Cap the number of AI-hardware names.
  - Prefer names whose momentum is backed by estimate increases over names driven by multiple expansion.
