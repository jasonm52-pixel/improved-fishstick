## 5. Historical seasonality (midterm years, Oct–Nov)

### Takeaway
Carson Group data (1950–2025) shows October (+3.0% average, 73.7% positive) and November (+2.7%, 78.9% positive) are the best two months of midterm years, averaging about +5.7% combined. September is weak. The sample is small (about 19 midterm years), so this is weak-to-moderate evidence.

### Cited Findings
- In midterm years 1950–2025 (Carson Group data): **October** averages **+3.0%**, positive in **73.7%** of years, ranked #1 of midterm-year months. **November** averages **+2.7%**, positive **78.9%**, ranked #2. **Oct+Nov combined averages about +5.7%**. June averages −2.1% (36.8% positive). September averages −0.8% (positive under 50% of the time) — [Yahoo/GuruFocus citing Carson Group, Sep 23 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/p-500-enters-historically-strong-192728409.html)
- Other items located but not fetched: [Seeking Alpha, "Midterm playbook: History favors a late-year lift in October and November"](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4645993-midterm-playbook-history-favors-a-late-year-lift-in-october-and-november) (robots-blocked); [Jeff Hirsch/Stock Trader's Almanac 2026 midterm seasonal pattern (blog repost)](https://time-price-research-astrofin.blogspot.com/2025/12/2026-midterm-election-year-seasonal.html); [IBKR Campus, S&P 500 seasonality and presidential cycles for 2026](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/technical-analysis/sp-500-seasonality-and-presidential-cycles-what-historical-patterns-suggest-for-2026/); [Barchart, "Q4 seasonality feels different… major overhang for markets in 2026"](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/36406308/q4-seasonality-feels-different-dont-miss-this-major-overhang-for-markets-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Percentages of 73.7% and 78.9% correspond to 14/19 and 15/19, which implies n=19 midterm years (1950–2022). That is a small sample, and the drivers overlap (midterm-year lows often come in Sep/Oct after drawdowns). The 2026 setup differs: the index is near a record, not after a midterm-year drawdown (outside the March oil shock), and the Fed is hiking. The seasonal tailwind is real on average but should be weighted lightly.
- The widely cited "post-midterm 12-month" strength (Carson/LPL) is outside this 8-week window. No sourced figure was retrieved in this pass.
